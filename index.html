Gluten-free kitchen rules

Read every label, every time

Formulas change without notice. Look for “Certified Gluten-Free” (GFCO, <10 ppm) or a “gluten-free” claim (FDA, <20 ppm). A “wheat-free” claim is not the same thing, because barley and rye also contain gluten.

Hidden-gluten watch list

  • Soy sauce (use GF tamari), teriyaki, hoisin
  • Broth, stock, bouillon
  • Sausage, marinated or pre-seasoned meats
  • Spice blends, taco/chili seasoning packets
  • BBQ sauce, marinades, Worcestershire (non-US)
  • Malt vinegar, beer
  • Cream-of-X soups, gravy mixes, enchilada sauce
  • Flavored rice mixes, oats (unless GF-certified)
  • Corn tortillas, cornmeal/polenta (cross-contact)
  • Some curry pastes and fish sauces

Stop cross-contact

  • Wash the colander well, or keep a GF-only one. Pasta starch clings to it.
  • Keep a separate toaster, or use toaster bags.
  • Replace scratched wooden spoons and cutting boards, or keep GF-only ones.
  • Squeeze-bottle condiments beat jars that knives dip into.
  • Cook GF pasta in fresh water in a clean pot.
  • Thicken with cornstarch, never all-purpose flour.
  • Wash the Instant Pot lid, sealing ring and float valve after any non-GF use.

Big Instant Pot + small batch

  • These recipes are sized at 4 servings partly so a large pot has enough in it. Halving them in a 6–8 qt pot risks a Burn error.
  • Pressure needs thin liquid: about 1–1.5 cups for a 6 qt, up to 2 cups for an 8 qt (check your manual). If yours is an 8 qt and it won't seal, add ½ cup broth.
  • Always deglaze after sautéing: scrape every browned bit off the bottom.
  • Layer thick foods (tomatoes, BBQ sauce, coconut milk) on top and don't stir. Add cream and cornstarch after pressure cooking.
  • “Burn” notice: cancel, release, scrape the bottom, add ½ cup water, and restart.
  • Total times include about 10–15 min to come up to pressure.

Big slow cooker + small batch

  • A half-empty large crock runs hotter and cooks faster, so start checking at the shortest time listed.
  • Keep the lid on. Each peek adds about 15–20 min.
  • Chicken is done at 165 °F. Pork shoulder and chuck roast need to go well past done (~200 °F) to shred.

Leftovers

  • Refrigerate within 2 hours and eat within 3–4 days.
  • Freeze well: chili, pulled pork, carnitas, tortilla soup, pot roast.
  • Don't freeze well: cream-and-potato soups (they get grainy).
